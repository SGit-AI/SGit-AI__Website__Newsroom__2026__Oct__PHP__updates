The state of affairs on 29 October: one vendor reports 25 servers rooted, unconfirmed, and still no named victim

Dream Research, a security vendor, reported on 26 October that a chain of the two php2shell flaws and a Linux kernel flaw rooted 25 servers, three in a US government-affiliated environment. It names no victim and no firewall vendor, publishes no evidence, and the three outlets that repeat it trace to its post; The Next Web says it has not verified it. It is carried as reported, not confirmed. No organisation has said it was compromised through php2shell at the evidence vault's latest capture, 00:09 UTC on 29 October.

The line

Exploited, on CISA's evidence; compromised, reported by one vendor and not confirmed; a named victim, still none. The lead measure for a named victim did not move: no organisation has said it was compromised through these flaws. What moved is what stands beside it: for the first time a vendor reports victims, unnamed. This version changes that, and the vendor-claims measure it belongs to, and carries every other measure from version 5 with its own as-of time.

Since version 5

  • The vault holds 681 sources at 00:09 UTC on 29 October (645 at version 5). The journalist's refreshes of 28 October, batches 25 to 28, found no new source naming an organisation compromised.
  • Four sources are new for this version: Dream Research's post of 26 October, and The Hacker News, The Next Web and CyPro, all of 28 October, repeating it. They were captured at 00:07 to 00:09 UTC on 29 October. Dream Research THN The Next Web CyPro

A vendor reports victims: reported, not confirmed

Dream Research, which sells the tooling it says it used, says a campaign chaining CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619 with a Linux kernel flaw, CVE-2026-46300, rooted 25 servers in the United States, Japan, Taiwan and South Korea: 24 firewalls of a large vendor it does not name and one internal firewall-management server, three of them in one US government-affiliated environment. It dates its find to 25 October and its post to 26 October, says the vendor was notified, names no actor, and says it cannot tell whether its later steps against the organisation's directory worked. Dream Research

What makes it a report and not a confirmation:

  • One source. The Hacker News, The Next Web and CyPro repeat Dream; CyPro's bulletin ends "Originally reported by securityweek.com". Three outlets, one origin. THN CyPro
  • No victim, no vendor, no evidence published. Dream's artifacts are not public, and no statement from the firewall vendor, CISA, the US organisation or the PHP team about these victims is in the evidence.
  • The press's own caveats. The Next Web: "TNW has not independently verified Dream's findings." CyPro: other sources do not independently verify Dream's victim details, and SecurityWeek reported two servers at the organisation where Dream says three, a difference it calls unresolved. The Next Web CyPro
  • Dream's own text is loose on its count: whether the second government firewall is one of the 24 or the 25th is not clear from the page.

What it does match: the two php2shell CVE numbers are the ones in CISA's listing. The kernel flaw's number is in no CISA material in the evidence. CISA

Vendor claims of a break-in: three made, two withdrawn

Version 5 said both vendor claims of a successful compromise had been withdrawn: Sophos on 22 October and Palo Alto Networks' Unit 42 on 25 October. That stays true of those two. Dream's report of 26 October is a third claim of successful compromise by a vendor, not withdrawn and not confirmed. Patchstack's incident reports from hosting partners are still not reconciled with its own telemetry. Cyber Security News SC Media Recorded Future

Compromise: still none named

At 00:09 UTC on 29 October, across 681 sources, no named organisation has said it was compromised through php2shell. A vendor's report of unnamed victims is not an organisation saying so, and it does not end that statement; the compromise page now carries it as a dated row beside the statement. Exploited is CISA's finding about the flaws; compromised needs a victim, or the vendor, or CISA, to say so.

Exploitation: the listing stands

CISA's listing of both CVEs on 26 October stands as last captured (19:13 UTC on 27 October); The Next Web and CyPro describe it as in place on 28 October. It names no victim and does not confirm Dream's account. CISA KEV The Next Web

Carried from version 5

These measures were not re-checked for this version and keep their own as-of times: the named incidents still open (four, as of 19:13 UTC on 27 October), the scores, the public code, Metasploit, the NCSC-NL inventory, the security vendors' product count, the end-of-life share, the national exposure count, the offline count and the authorities that know of no successful exploitation (five).

What this version changed on the site

The reader question "Has anyone actually been hacked through php2shell?" has a dated update. The compromise page and the story carry Dream's report as reported, not confirmed. The "been hacked" answers on the front page and the questions page name it and move their as-of to 00:09 UTC on 29 October.

What would change this state

  • A named organisation, the firewall vendor, CISA or the US government-affiliated organisation confirming Dream's account.
  • Dream publishing evidence others can check, or a second source that did not start from its post.
  • A named organisation confirming a compromise, most likely from its own search.
  • Any of the four open incidents stating php2shell as the cause.
  • A merged Metasploit module.
  • The NCSC-NL inventory moving again.
