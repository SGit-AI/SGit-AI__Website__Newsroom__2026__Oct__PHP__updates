## The line

Exploited, on CISA's evidence; compromised, reported by one vendor and not confirmed; a named victim, still none. The lead measure for a named victim did not move: no organisation has said it was compromised through these flaws. What moved is what stands beside it: for the first time a vendor reports victims, unnamed. This version changes that, and the vendor-claims measure it belongs to, and carries every other measure from version 5 with its own as-of time.

## A vendor reports victims: reported, not confirmed

Dream Research, which sells the tooling it says it used, says a campaign chaining CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619 with a Linux kernel flaw, CVE-2026-46300, rooted 25 servers in the United States, Japan, Taiwan and South Korea: 24 firewalls of a large vendor it does not name and one internal firewall-management server, three of them in one US government-affiliated environment. It dates its find to 25 October and its post to 26 October, says the vendor was notified, names no actor, and says it cannot tell whether its later steps against the organisation's directory worked. [Dream Research](https://refs.php.oct.2026.newsroom.sgit.ai/sources/dreamgroup-chained-php2shell-roots-25-servers/)

What makes it a report and not a confirmation:

- One source. The Hacker News, The Next Web and CyPro repeat Dream; CyPro's bulletin ends "Originally reported by securityweek.com". Three outlets, one origin. [THN](https://refs.php.oct.2026.newsroom.sgit.ai/sources/the-hacker-news-thehackernews-php2shell-roots-25-s/) [CyPro](https://refs.php.oct.2026.newsroom.sgit.ai/sources/cypro-php2shell-exploit-roots-firewalls/)

- No victim, no vendor, no evidence published. Dream's artifacts are not public, and no statement from the firewall vendor, CISA, the US organisation or the PHP team about these victims is in the evidence.

- The press's own caveats. The Next Web: "TNW has not independently verified Dream's findings." CyPro: other sources do not independently verify Dream's victim details, and SecurityWeek reported two servers at the organisation where Dream says three, a difference it calls unresolved. [The Next Web](https://refs.php.oct.2026.newsroom.sgit.ai/sources/the-next-web-php2shell-cisa-kev-dream-25-servers/) [CyPro](https://refs.php.oct.2026.newsroom.sgit.ai/sources/cypro-php2shell-exploit-roots-firewalls/)

- Dream's own text is loose on its count: whether the second government firewall is one of the 24 or the 25th is not clear from the page.

What it does match: the two php2shell CVE numbers are the ones in CISA's listing. The kernel flaw's number is in no CISA material in the evidence. [CISA](https://refs.php.oct.2026.newsroom.sgit.ai/sources/cisa-kev-json/)

## Compromise: still none named

At 00:09 UTC on 29 October, across 681 sources, no named organisation has said it was compromised through php2shell. A vendor's report of unnamed victims is not an organisation saying so, and it does not end that statement; the compromise page now carries it as a dated row beside the statement. Exploited is CISA's finding about the flaws; compromised needs a victim, or the vendor, or CISA, to say so.

## Exploitation: the listing stands

CISA's listing of both CVEs on 26 October stands as last captured (19:13 UTC on 27 October); The Next Web and CyPro describe it as in place on 28 October. ## What would change this state

- A named organisation, the firewall vendor, CISA or the US government-affiliated organisation confirming Dream's account.

- Dream publishing evidence others can check, or a second source that did not start from its post.

- A named organisation confirming a compromise, most likely from its own search.

- Any of the four open incidents stating php2shell as the cause.

- A merged Metasploit module.

- The NCSC-NL inventory moving again.
