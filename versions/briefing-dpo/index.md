Briefing for Morgan, data protection officer · ******Some technical # Was personal data exposed, does this start a GDPR breach assessment or a 72-hour notification, and what do I document? M The answer in thirty seconds A flaw is not a breach. An unpatched, internet-facing PHP application holding personal data is a risk to record and treat; it becomes a breach to assess when there is a sign of compromise on it. No named organisation has said it was compromised through php2shell as of the last check. Ask your hosts in writing, review from 17 October, and document the assessment either way. Read the site as Morgan → Four questions, in order: is personal data on a system that was exposed, has anything happened to it, does that make it a personal data breach, and what goes on the record. A vulnerability is not a breach, and an outage you chose is not one either; a sign that someone ran code on a system holding personal data is where the assessment starts. Written by the GRC desk; the full register is at GRC. ## What to decide or do - **Find the personal data first.** Ask the CTO or security lead for the list of internet-facing PHP systems (vendor products and hosting panels count), and mark which hold or reach personal data: accounts, orders, case files, the database the application logs into. Code execution on the server gives an attacker what the PHP process can read, usually including the database credentials in its configuration. - **Exposed is not breached.** An unpatched system is a risk under Article 32 to treat and record, not a breach under Article 33. As of the last check, no named organisation has said it was compromised through php2shell; attempts are widely reported and two vendors report successful exploitation without naming a victim. What that statement rests on. - **When the 72-hour clock starts.** Under Article 33 it runs from when you become aware, with reasonable certainty, that personal data was affected: a web shell, a PHP worker spawning a shell, an unexplained database export on a system holding personal data. Ask the security lead to tell you the hour that happens, not the day after, and start the assessment then. - **A precautionary shutdown is a decision, not a breach.** Taking a service offline on purpose to protect it is not an accidental or unlawful loss of availability. Record who decided, when, for how long, and that no personal data was lost. - **Ask your processors in writing.** A host or managed platform is a processor under Article 28 and must tell you of a breach without undue delay (Article 33(2)). Ask each one: have you updated PHP on the systems that process our data, since when, have you seen any sign of compromise, and will you tell us within hours if you do? Keep the replies. - **Document either way.** Article 33(5) asks you to record every breach; accountability under Article 5(2) asks you to show your reasoning when you decide something is not one. Record: the systems and the personal data on each, the date each was patched or isolated, the log review window (from 17 October, the earliest reported attempt, not from the 21st), what was found, and the decision with its reasons and who made it. - **If it becomes a breach.** Notify the supervisory authority within 72 hours unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to people (Article 33), and tell the people affected without undue delay if the risk to them is high (Article 34).
