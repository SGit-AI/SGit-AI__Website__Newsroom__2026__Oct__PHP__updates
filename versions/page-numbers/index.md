It includes every site on a shared host that patched hours ago, every site behind a CDN, and every site that has not been touched since 2019. It is an upper bound on exposure. - **Internet-facing hosts that expose PHP** (2.6 million, one scanner's count) is a count of machines, not sites. One host serves many sites; most sites sit behind hosts that do not announce PHP at all. It is a lower bound on the machines an attacker could reach directly. - **Websites that could be affected** (30 million) has no stated basis. It may be a fraction of a websites total; it may be a guess. - **Resources potentially affected in one country** (about 6,000 in Latvia) is the only figure from a body that could inventory what it counts. Scaled naively by population it would put the EU in the low millions, which is a different number from any of the above and is not offered here as one. - **Products in an inventory** (674) counts software, not deployments: one row is WordPress, another is a Linux distribution's package. The number a reader actually wants, how many systems were exploitable at a given hour and how many were exploited, does not exist in any captured source.
