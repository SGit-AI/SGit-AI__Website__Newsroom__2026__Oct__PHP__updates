Briefing · CTO # What does this cost us in engineering time, what breaks when we patch, and what do we change so the next one is a non-event? What the patch changes, what it breaks, and what to change afterwards. The fix is a redesign of how PHP handles running out of memory, so behaviour under memory pressure changes, and it touched 389 files. ## What to decide or do - Patch order: mod_php on the internet first; FPM next; everything else after. Restart every worker, rebuild every image, then verify the running version inside the container. - The new memory_hard_limit_percent and max_multipart_body_parts settings exist now; the defaults are conservative, and a system that used to survive on the edge of memory_limit may now _exit instead. - 7.4, 8.0 and 8.1 have a source backport and no release: that is a migration you are now doing, not a patch. - The engineering lesson is time-to-pull-the-plug: how long it takes to take one system off the internet cleanly, with a holding page and a way back, is a property of your platform and it was measured this week whether you wanted it or not. - What to change: an inventory that answers "where is PHP" in minutes; worker-process monitoring (a PHP worker spawning a shell is the signal); a rehearsed isolate runbook with a named owner. ## The facts this rests on - confirmed two or more primary sources agree, or the fact was counted from a captured file - reported one party says so, and it has not been contradicted - disputed the sources disagree, and both readings are shown - contradicted a primary source says otherwise - unverified even the reporting outlet could not check it - p/n primary sources among the n cited;
