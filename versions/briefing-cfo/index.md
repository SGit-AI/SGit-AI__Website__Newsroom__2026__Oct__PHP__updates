Briefing · CFO # What is the financial exposure, what does downtime cost against breach cost, and will insurance respond? The financial question has two sides and the evidence is lopsided: downtime is documented and measurable; breach is claimed and unconfirmed. Insurers are already treating this as an aggregation event. ## What to decide or do - Cost of acting: operators that pulled sites report hours to a day offline; one operator served 94% of its page views from cache through a 13-hour shutdown. Engineering time to patch, restart and verify is the main cost for most. - Cost of not acting: an unauthenticated code-execution flaw in the web tier; what it reaches is whatever that tier can reach, which is a question for the CISO's inventory, not for a percentage. - Insurance: Insurance Business reports underwriters watching for an aggregation event and asks whether business-interruption cover responds to a precautionary shutdown with no breach; that depends on wording and waiting periods. Ask the broker now, in writing. - Figures you will hear and should not use in a forecast: 70% of the Internet (a share of websites restated), 30 million websites (no basis), 22.6 million systems (method not given). Use your own inventory count. - No named organisation has confirmed a compromise as of the latest capture.
