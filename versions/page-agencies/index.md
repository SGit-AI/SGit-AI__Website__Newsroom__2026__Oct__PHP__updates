# What sixteen agencies actually advise The NSA, CISA, the UK NCSC, CERT-EU, ENISA, CERT-FR, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, the Netherlands, Italy, Switzerland, Latvia, Japan, Spain and Portugal on php2shell: who says take it offline, who says isolate, who says patch and nothing more, and who has seen exploitation. # The agencies, side by side 16 national and supranational bodies, each read from its own page. The question that divides them is not whether to patch (all say patch) but what to do with a system that cannot be patched now. Of 16: 6 say *none*, 2 say *yes*, 2 say *isolate*, 2 say *unknown*, 1 say *after mitigations*, 1 say *isolate first*, 1 say *consider*, 1 say *restrict from abroad*. Two could not be read because their sites block automated browsers; the block is recorded. The BBC's morning framing, *the UK's NCSC, the NSA and their counterparts in the EU and Australia advise taking systems offline*, holds for the NSA and CISA, is a simplification of the NCSC's fourth of six steps, and is not supported by the Australian page at all.
